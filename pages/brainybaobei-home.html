01 / Your BaZi chart

The four pillars

Each pillar pairs a heavenly stem with an earthly branch. The day stem—your Day Master—is the reference point, not your whole identity.

Hover to explore. Tap or press Enter to keep an explanation open.

Single arrows show generation and control. Double arrows show pairings. A stem pair may both combine and have an element-control relationship; this does not establish transformation. These are traditional relationships between symbols, not predictions of conflict or harm.

Complete three-branch groups are required for three-way punishment here. Partial groups and transformations are not inferred.

Hidden stems & reading the chart

The relationship names are traditional terms, not literal judgments. “Rob Wealth” does not mean theft; “Seven Killings” does not mean violence. Hidden stems are shown separately from the eight visible symbols.

How to read these bars

One count per visible stem and per branch’s main element. Hidden stems are counted separately, without weights. A larger bar means more occurrences—not a stronger personality or a healthier element. Season, roots and transformations matter to a full strength assessment; this chart does not calculate a useful element or prescribe a remedy.

Love & Relationships

The pattern we are working with

Independence can become a contest over who gets the final say.

Ask, then reflect back

Explore where companionship supports your independence, and where comparison or competition gets in the way of listening. In one ordinary conversation, ask what kind of support is wanted. Reflect back what you heard before offering a solution.

Your space · an optional experiment

Create a comfortable place for an undistracted conversation using what you already own.

Outfit & personal expression

For a conversation that matters, wear something comfortable enough to forget about. Notice whether you feel freer to listen and speak when you are not monitoring how you look.

A mindset to practise

Treat “independence & belonging” as something to work with, not a label you must live up to. What would you like another person to understand more clearly?

Your step-by-step path

  1. Notice one recent example of this pattern, plus one situation where the opposite was true.
  2. Choose an outcome for this experiment: ask, then reflect back. Keep it small and observable.
  3. Try the daily action once. If you want, test a space or outfit change separately so you can tell what helped.
  4. Did the other person confirm that you understood, or correct your assumption?
  5. Keep, adapt or stop the experiment. Repeat only what is useful, then choose one next step—not a complete reinvention.
Wealth

Outfit & personal expression

Career

The pattern we are working with

Define a useful finish

Compare the freedom to make decisions with the support you receive from colleagues. Which is missing in your current environment? For one small task, write what “finished” means and what is outside its scope. Check that understanding with anyone relying on it.

Your space · an optional experiment

Keep the materials for that one task within reach and put unrelated work out of the immediate workspace.

Outfit & personal expression

Choose clothes for the work you will actually do today: moving, meeting or concentrating. Separate a practical requirement from an unspoken expectation about looking successful.

Wellbeing

The pattern we are working with

Trying to keep up socially can leave little unstructured time.

Protect an ordinary pause

Notice which social settings leave room for you to be ordinary, rather than impressive. This is not a health assessment. Choose a brief, comfortable pause in an existing routine. Leave it free of output, comparison or self-assessment. Stop if the exercise is not helpful.

Your space · an optional experiment

Try a quieter, comfortable corner with safe lighting and clear access. This is not a health treatment.

Outfit & personal expression

At the end of a demanding day, try changing into clothing that feels physically comfortable. Notice whether this helps mark the transition out of work; it is not a health intervention.

A mindset to practise

Treat “independence & belonging” as something to work with, not a label you must live up to. Where could you make room for ordinary recovery?

Your step-by-step path

Personal Growth

The pattern we are working with

Borrowed goals can crowd out the direction you would choose privately.

Learn from a small beginning

Distinguish a goal you chose from a goal that became attractive because somebody else wanted it. Choose a reversible activity you have been postponing. Give it one short session, then write down what doing it taught you.

Your space · an optional experiment

Leave one simple cue for the activity where you will notice it, without making the space harder to use.

Outfit & personal expression

Revisit one piece you like but rarely wear. Try it in a low-stakes setting and decide from your own experience whether it still expresses something you value.
