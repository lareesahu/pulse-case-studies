About

Why "Ancient Science of China"?

Every civilization develops ways of observing, organizing, and transmitting knowledge. The Chinese intellectual tradition spans over three millennia of systematic inquiry — into nature, time, mathematics, medicine, astronomy, and the patterns that connect them.

Yet for English-speaking audiences, this tradition is often fragmented across scattered Wikipedia entries, academic journals, and museum exhibits. There is no single authoritative source that introduces the full breadth of Chinese classical knowledge with the seriousness it deserves.

Ancient Science of China exists to fill that gap.

We use "science" in its broader sense — systematic inquiry into nature, time, humanity, and reality — not as an attempt to retrofit Chinese traditions into modern categories, but because the intellectual practices we catalogue were rigorous, cumulative, and self-correcting. They were debated, refined, and transmitted across generations of scholars working within institutions that demanded precision.

What We Catalogue

The project is organized around the classical Chinese knowledge taxonomy — astronomy and calendrics, medicine, mathematics, natural philosophy, and the fate studies traditions — extended to encompass the full range of systematic inquiry in pre-modern China. Every entry is researched from primary sources and scholarly references.

What We Are Not

We are not a "wisdom" site, a nationalist heritage project, or a spirituality destination. We do not claim that every ancient text contains hidden truths. We claim only this: Chinese knowledge traditions deserve to be understood before they are judged.

A Note on Language

Some pages on this site carry substantial Chinese text alongside the English. This is deliberate — not an oversight. In the course of assembling primary sources, classical quotations, and modern Chinese scholarship, we repeatedly encountered concepts that have no neat English equivalent. The nuance carried by a term like qi (气), li (理), or jingluo (经络) is flattened by any single-word translation, and the classical texts that house these ideas often resist paraphrase without loss. Rather than pretend a tidy English rendering always suffices, we present both languages side by side — so that when English reaches its limit, you have the original Chinese at hand to consult with a translation tool, an AI assistant, or a Chinese-literate colleague. Bilingualism here is not decoration; it is an honest response to the problem of translating a knowledge tradition into another linguistic world.

The Name

The word "ancient" signals the historical scope without confining the project to antiquity. It encompasses the full pre-modern intellectual tradition from Shang through Qing. "Science" is used in its broader, older sense — scientia, systematic knowledge. The About page on the full site will explore this choice in depth, acknowledging the term's modern connotations while arguing for its applicability.

Colophon

Ancient Science of China was founded as a personal research project. The catalogue is freely accessible — every masterpiece, scholar, and institution can be read without accounts, paywalls, or restrictions. An open API is available for researchers, educators, and application developers.

For corrections, suggestions, or collaboration: the project repository is at github.com/lareesahu/ancient-science-of-china.
